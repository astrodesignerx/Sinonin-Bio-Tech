03
Beyond the numbers

The four I most wanted to fix.

Speed was the easy part. These four cost you enquiries directly, and not one of them is visible from the homepage, which is why they have gone unnoticed.

1  ·  Market Reports: your commercial page is empty

The hub page shows a stale "4 Oct, 2022 · Administrator · 0" byline and no reports at all. The four report pages beneath it do have working order forms, asking for name, email, phone, company and job title: a properly designed lead capture. So the machinery for selling reports already exists; the page standing in front of it is dead, and the forms sit two levels down a menu where a buyer has to go looking. Of everything in this document, this is the one I would have fixed first.

2  ·  The blog is one page of 26,429 words

The blog index does not list your articles. It prints six of them in full, end to end, on a single address. 103,216 pixels tall, roughly 127 phone screens of scrolling. Nobody reaches the sixth piece. It also places six H1 headings on one page, which tells Google the index and the real articles are competing, and splits the ranking you have earned between them. The writing is the best asset you have, and the current setup buries it.

3  ·  A German company, invisible in German

The site is English-only and declares no German alternative to search engines whatsoever. For a GmbH registered in Langwedel, selling into European petfood manufacturing, every German-language search for insect protein, palatants or formulation support currently resolves to a competitor. This is the largest untapped audience you have, and it costs nothing extra to reach.

4  ·  Product images at 71 × 108 pixels

The packshot on the insect protein page is natively 71 pixels wide, stretched to fill its slot, visibly soft next to the brands it is meant to represent. Your logo, meanwhile, ships at 1,280px into a 216px space, roughly 35× more data than needed. On that same page only four of eight images loaded at all, against five brands named in the copy. You sell scientific precision, and the pictures should not be the thing undercutting it.

What this changes

Eight things you get that you do not have today.

01

Your reports become buyable

A working Market Reports hub, plus a dedicated page per report with its own request form. The revenue path that is currently blocked by an empty page is open.

02

You can sell in German

The full site in both languages, not a translated homepage. A German buyer reads German, and Google can finally see that you serve the market you are registered in.

03

A compliance gap closes

Your report form collects name, email, phone, company and job title with no consent step, and no German privacy policy behind it. Now: a consent checkbox on every form, and Datenschutz in both languages.

04

Your writing works as credibility

Each article gets its own page, cover image and summary card. Individually shareable, individually rankable, and readable on a phone, instead of buried in a 26,000-word wall.

05

The homepage makes a claim

A single clear proposition in the first screen, then a deliberate path: the four expertise areas, the protein-security case, reports, training, and who you are.

06

It works properly on a phone

No sideways scrolling, no 10px text, tap targets sized for thumbs. This matters most where you already share your work. LinkedIn traffic is overwhelmingly mobile.

07

Shares carry your brand

Every link you post currently pulls a funder's logo as its picture. Now each page has its own branded share image, title and description.

08

Nothing to maintain or patch

No plugin stack, no WordPress updates, no security surface to keep an eye on. Nothing to break while you are not looking.

What happens to the rankings you already haveContinuity

A rebuild normally costs you your search position: every address changes, and Google treats the new pages as strangers. That does not happen here. Fifteen redirect rules map every old address to its replacement, including the tag, category and author archives WordPress generated on your behalf. Anyone arriving on an old link, whether from a search result or a citation in someone else's article, lands on the right page rather than an error.
